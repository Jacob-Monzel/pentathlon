# Swim — Foundation Block

**Purpose:** get you from "25 metres kills me" to the point where a real 100m program makes sense.
**Duration:** ~10 weeks, 3 swims/week, 45 min each.
**Pool:** Pershing Field (25 yd) — weekdays 7:00 AM–6:30 PM, Sat/Sun 12:30–5:00 PM. Penn as available.

This is temporary by design. When you clear the four gates at the bottom, this block retires and we build
the actual 100m race program — race-pace ladder, 4×50 benchmark, split targets, UIPM points.

---

## The one thing that matters more than this document

**Book lessons.** Pershing Field runs adult classes. Stroke mechanics is one of the few things you
genuinely cannot self-coach, because you can't see what you're doing and the errors feel normal from the
inside. Six sessions with a coach will beat ten weeks of solo work following this. Use this document to
structure the swims *between* lessons, not instead of them.

---

## Every session looks the same

Repetition is the point. You're building a motor pattern, and variety works against that.

| Block | Duration | What |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | ~5 min | 200 easy, any stroke, no agenda |
| **Focus** | ~10 min | Drills for the current 2-week block (below) |
| Main set | ~20 min | Short reps, form-first, generous rest |
| Cool-down | ~5 min | 100 easy |

Total ≈ 40–45 min. If you finish early, stop early. Padding the session with tired laps is how you
groove bad habits.

---

## The main set, and how it progresses

**Start here:** 8 × 25 yd, 30–45 s rest between each.

Count your strokes on **one length per session** — pick a rep in the middle of the set, count arm
entries, remember the number. That's your whole tracking burden.

**The progression rule:**

- All reps done with stroke count within **±2** and form intact → **add a rep** next session (up to 12)
- 12 × 25 clean → move up to **6 × 50**
- 8 × 50 clean → **4 × 100**
- 4 × 100 clean → **continuous 200**

**The stopping rule:** if your stroke count climbs by 3 or more, the set is over. Not "push through" —
over. Reps past that point are teaching your body the wrong pattern, which you'll then have to unlearn.

**Why stroke count and not the clock:** your 100 time will barely move for months, but your efficiency
will improve within weeks. Stroke count shows you that. It also can't be gamed by thrashing — you can
always swim a length faster by working harder, but you can't fake taking fewer strokes.

## Focus blocks — 2 weeks each, in this order

### Weeks 1–2 · Body position

*Why first:* drag beats propulsion at your stage. A fit runner usually has dense, sinky legs and a
head-up posture, and fixing that is worth more than any amount of pulling harder.

- **Superman glide** — push off the wall, arms extended, face down, hold streamline and glide until you
  stop. 8–10 reps. Feel where the waterline sits on your head.
- **Streamline push-offs** — hands stacked, one over the other, arms squeezing your ears. Every single
  push-off for the rest of your life should look like this.
- **Side-kick balance** — on your side, bottom arm extended, top arm along your hip, looking straight
  down. Gentle kick, 25 yd at a time. This is the hardest of the three and the most valuable.

*Cue:* press your chest very slightly down — "swim downhill." Look at the bottom of the pool, not
forward. If your legs sink, it's almost always because your head is too high.

### Weeks 3–4 · Breathing

*Why second:* this is almost certainly why 25 yd feels lethal. You have the aerobic fitness to run for
an hour. What's happening in the pool is breath-holding, then panic-inhaling with your head lifted.

- **Bobbing** — in the shallow end, sink, hum a continuous exhale out through your nose and mouth,
  surface, quick inhale. 20 reps. Boring, transformative.
- **Side-kick breathing** — from the side-kick position, rotate just enough to breathe with your *lower*
  goggle still in the water.
- **Swim with a 2-stroke pattern** to your comfortable side. Don't force bilateral yet — that's a
  refinement for later, not a beginner requirement.

*Cue:* **exhale continuously the entire time your face is in the water.** Never hold. The inhale should
be small and quick — you're topping up, not gasping. Rotate to breathe, don't lift.

### Weeks 5–6 · Turns

*Why here rather than last:* it's a 25 yd pool, so a 100 is four lengths with **three turns**. That's
roughly a quarter of your race spent doing something you currently can't do. It's not a finishing touch.

- **Open turn first** — touch the wall, tuck your knees in, drop one shoulder, pivot your feet onto the
  wall, push off on your side into streamline, rotate to your front as you glide. Legal, low-risk, and a
  well-executed open turn costs you very little. Get this solid before touching flip turns.
- **Flip turn progression** — (1) somersaults in open water; (2) somersault a few feet off the wall
  without touching it; (3) somersault, plant both feet, push off on your back into streamline, rotate to
  front during the glide; (4) the whole thing at slow swimming speed.

*Cue:* don't reach for the wall and stop. Keep swimming into it — the turn is a somersault that happens
to have a wall in the way.

### Weeks 7–8 · Catch and pull

*Why now:* propulsion only becomes worth optimising once position and breathing aren't costing you.

- **Catch-up drill** — one arm stays extended out front until the other hand touches it. Slows
  everything down and forces a full extension.
- **Fingertip drag** — during recovery, drag your fingertips along the surface. Teaches a high elbow
  without you having to think about your elbow.
- **Sculling** — arms out front, forearms angled down, small figure-eight sculls. Teaches you what
  pressure on the forearm actually feels like.

*Cue:* reach forward, then bend the elbow and press the water **backward with the whole forearm**.
Beginners push *down* with a straight arm, which lifts them up instead of moving them forward.

### Weeks 9–10 · Kick and ankles

*Why last:* the kick contributes relatively little propulsion in freestyle, but a bad one adds a lot of
drag. Runners and lifters almost always have stiff ankles, which turns the kick into a brake.

- **Vertical kicking** — deep end, arms crossed on your chest, kick to keep your chin at the surface.
  4 × 20–30 s. Brutal and extremely honest.
- **Kick on your side**, board optional, from the week 1–2 position.
- **Ankle mobility on deck** — seated, legs straight out, point and circle the ankles, 2 × 30 s. *Do the
  seated version rather than the kneeling toes-pointed stretch — the kneeling one loads the knee in deep
  flexion, which isn't worth it given your knee history.*

*Cue:* kick from the hip, not the knee. Small, fast, relaxed, floppy ankles. Toes just breaking the
surface. If you can hear a lot of splashing, the kick is too big.

**After week 10:** cycle back through, two weeks each, starting from body position. The drills don't get
stale — your execution of them gets better.

## What to log in the app

Just **minutes and session RPE.** That's it — it feeds weekly load, monotony, and strain alongside your
lifting, running, and fencing, which is the only thing the app actually needs from a swim right now.

Optionally drop the focus and stroke count in the note field:

```
breathing · 17 str/25
```

Don't bother with the segment builder for these. It's built for structured race sessions, and you'll
have those soon enough.

## Graduation gates

Clear all four and the foundation block is done. Come back and we build the real 100m program.

- [ ] **Continuous 100 yd** without the breathing pattern falling apart
- [ ] **Stroke count within ±2** across 6 × 25
- [ ] **A legal turn** — open or flip — repeatable when you're tired
- [ ] **800–1,000 yd** of quality work inside 45 minutes

Realistic timeline: 8–12 weeks at 3 swims a week. Faster with lessons.

## Two things to remember for later

**Yards vs metres.** You train in a 25 yd pool but race 100 **metres**. 100 yd ≈ 91.4 m, so a yards time
flatters you by several seconds. When we wire up UIPM points, the app will only compute them from a
genuine 100 m swim rather than guessing from yards — an encouraging fake number is worse than none.

**The scoring, when you get there.** 1:10.0 = 250 points, and every 0.2 s either way is worth 1 point.
So `points = 250 + (70 − your time in seconds) × 5`.
